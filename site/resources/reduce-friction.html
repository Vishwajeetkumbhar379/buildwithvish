Reduce friction: the setup before your first client

Three Claude prompts that turn your CV into a freelance profile, a one-page portfolio and a 4-week plan. The checklist from Week 2 of my challenge.

Guide and checklist, 10 minutes

In 10 minutes you'll have the three things I'm setting up before I chase a single client: a freelance profile, a one-page portfolio and a plan for the next four weeks. Claude writes the first drafts from your CV. You fix them.

Works with any Claude plan, the free one included. Checked 27 Oct 2026.
@buildwith.vish · October 2026

1. What you are building

220 companies said no to me, so I'm building my own job. Week 2 of my 52-week challenge starts with one rule: reduce friction first. Friction is anything that stands between you and a yes. If someone wants to hire you today, they need somewhere to find you, proof you can do the work, and you need to know what you're selling.

So you'll build three things: a profile on the freelancing platforms you pick, a portfolio page with your best work, and a plan for your content and income. Claude drafts each one from what you paste in. Nothing is posted anywhere until you post it.

3. Setup in three steps

Step 1. Pick two places to be found. Don't sign up everywhere. Pick the two where your buyers already look: a freelance marketplace (Upwork, Fiverr, Malt in Europe or Contra) and your LinkedIn profile. Make the accounts, leave them empty.

Step 2. Give Claude your facts once. Start a Claude Project called "My offer" (or one long chat) and paste in your CV and your three pieces of work. Every prompt below then works from the same facts.

Step 3. Run the three prompts, then fix the drafts by hand. Read each draft out loud. Anything you wouldn't say to a friend on a call goes. Anything Claude made up goes too: it only gets the facts you gave it.

4. Prompt templates

Swap the parts in [square brackets].

  1. The profile: Using only the facts in my CV, write my profile for [platform]. Give me a headline under 70 characters that says who I help and with what, an overview of 120 to 150 words that opens with the client's problem, not my job title, and 10 skills I can prove. Don't invent numbers, clients or results. If something is missing, ask me instead.
  2. The portfolio: Turn each of my three projects into a short case study for a one-page portfolio: the problem, what I did in 3 steps, and the result with the real number I gave you. 80 words each, plain English, first person. Then suggest the order to show them in, for someone hiring a [role].
  3. The plan: I want to earn [amount] a month from [skill] within [time]. Based on my CV, list 3 offers I could sell, who would buy each one, and what each should cost to start. Then give me a 4-week plan: what to post each week (2 posts a week), who to message, and one thing to finish each week. Keep it doable alongside [my job or studies].

7. Read this twice

  • Check that you're allowed to freelance before you take paid work. In Germany, for example, students from outside the EU need permission for self-employed work. I'm sorting mine out before I take a paid job.
  • Never put numbers, clients or logos in your portfolio that you can't show if someone asks.
  • Claude sees what you paste in. Remove anything private (addresses, salaries, client data) first.
  • Don't spend money before you earn it. A phone and a window are enough for your first videos.

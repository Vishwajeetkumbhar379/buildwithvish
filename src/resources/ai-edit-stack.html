1. What you are building

I filmed my Week 1 reel on my phone. I didn't open an editing app. Claude Code wrote and ran the edit as code: it read the transcript, cut the clips, built every caption, sticker and title as a small web page, rendered them frame by frame, laid the sounds under each visual and exported the file. My job was the footage and the feedback. The version I posted is the 12th draft, 1 minute 51 seconds long.

Everything runs on your computer. The only things that leave it are what you send to Claude (the transcript and the frames you ask it to look at) and the text you send to a voice tool, if you use one.

3. Setup in three steps

Step 1. Put the clips in one project folder. Make a folder per video, with raw/ for your clips and broll/ for any extra footage. Open Claude Code in that folder.

mkdir -p my-reel/raw my-reel/broll && cd my-reel && claude

Step 2. Install the tools. Ask Claude to check what's missing and install it. It will run the commands from the table above and test each one.

Check that ffmpeg, faster-whisper and playwright (with chromium) are installed. Install what's missing and show me each version.

Step 3. Run the five prompts below, in order. Look at the result after each one, and give feedback in plain words ("the cut at 0:12 is too early", "that sound is too loud"). That loop is the whole job.

4. Prompt templates

Swap the parts in [square brackets].

  1. Transcribe: Transcribe every clip in raw/ with faster-whisper, model [small], with word timestamps. Transcribe each stretch of speech separately so the word times don't drift over long silences. Save transcript.json.
  2. Cut plan: Using transcript.json, keep my best take of each line and drop false starts and repeats. Remove silences longer than [0.4] seconds, but never cut mid-sentence. Use one steady speed for the whole video ([1.1x] works for me), never speed ramps. Show me the cut list with the text of each line before you cut.
  3. Storyboard: For each kept line, propose what appears on screen: a sticker, a number, a title, or B-roll from broll/ that literally matches the words. One idea per line. Captions sit between 58% and 66% of the frame height, inside Instagram's safe zone (14% top, 35% bottom, 6% sides free). Show me the table first.
  4. Build and sound: Build each graphic as HTML, render it frame by frame with Playwright, and lay it over the cut with FFmpeg. Add one short sound to every visual event (a pop for stickers, a whoosh for transitions), keep my voice on top, and duck the music under speech. Leave true silence on [the line where I pause].
  5. Export: Render 1080x1920 at 30 fps, H.264, loudness around -14 LUFS. Also give me a small review copy with the time burned into the corner so I can give feedback by timestamp.

6. Troubleshooting

ProblemFix
The render crashes ("Target crashed")Usually a full disk. Free at least 3 GB, then render again.
Captions drift away from the wordsTranscribe each stretch of speech on its own (prompt 1 already says this).
Instagram's buttons cover the captionsMove them up into 58% to 66% of the height, away from the bottom 35%.
The music fights your voiceAsk Claude to duck the music by about 10 dB under speech, and not to pump it.
The cuts feel jumpyAsk for fewer cuts inside a sentence and one steady speed, no ramps. Natural takes beat tight ones.
B-roll doesn't fit the lineGive Claude a short note per clip ("me hiking, Black Forest") so it can match words to footage.

7. Read this twice

  • Claude Code needs a paid Claude plan. All the other tools here are free and open source.
  • ElevenLabs' free plan is not licensed for commercial use. Use a paid plan, or your own voice, for anything you monetise.
  • Don't bake copyrighted songs into the file. Add music from Instagram's own library when you post, or use tracks you have the rights to.
  • Meta asks you to label realistic AI-made audio or video. An AI narrator voice counts, so turn on the AI label when you use one.
  • Your clips stay on your machine. Claude sees the transcript and any frames you ask it to look at.
  • RVM is GPL-3.0: free to use, but if you share code that includes it, that code must be GPL too.

AI isn't only for work. Some of the most useful things it does are small and ordinary: turning a confusing letter into plain words, making dinner out of half a fridge, writing the message you've been avoiding. This page has 45 copy-paste prompts for normal life, grouped by area: home, food, money admin, letters and forms, health admin, travel, family and friends, learning, and decisions. Each one is ready to use in Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or any other chat app. Swap the brackets for your details and you're done. Skim the headings, pick one you need today, and try it in the next five minutes.

In short
  • 45 prompts for everyday life, each with a line on when to use it.
  • Replace everything in [square brackets] with your details. The more specific, the better the answer.
  • Most prompts ask the AI to check with you first or give a clear format, so answers come back usable.
  • For money, legal and health admin, AI explains and drafts. It doesn't replace a professional.
  • Never paste passwords, ID numbers or bank details. Remove them from letters before you share.

How to use these prompts

Fill the brackets. "[size] flat" becomes "two-room flat with a small kitchen". Specific details are what turn a generic answer into one that fits your life.

Add a line of context. Who you are, what you've tried, what you don't want. One sentence is often enough. For bigger tasks, use the six-box brief.

Attach instead of typing. Most AI chat apps let you add a photo or PDF, usually with a paperclip or plus button next to the message box. That's handy for letters, bills, menus and recipes. See the help pages for Claude and ChatGPT.

Follow up. The first answer is a draft. "Half the words, same meaning" or "That's too generic. Ask me 3 questions about my situation first, then rewrite it." usually fixes it. More in 12 follow-ups that fix weak AI answers.

Privacy

Before you paste or photograph anything, cover or delete passwords, ID and passport numbers, tax and insurance numbers, bank and card details, and other people's private information. The AI rarely needs them to help. To check what your app keeps and learns from, see Five privacy settings to check in your AI apps.

Home

A cleaning plan that keeps your weekend free

For when the flat only gets cleaned in one miserable Saturday push.

Make a cleaning plan for my [size, e.g. two-room] flat. I live with [who] and want no more than 20 minutes a day so the weekend stays free. I hate [chore]. Show it as a table by day of the week, and tell me which tasks I can safely do less often than I think.

Declutter one room in an afternoon

Gives you an order to work in, so you don't stall halfway with everything on the floor.

Help me declutter my [room] in one afternoon. Ask me 3 questions first about what's in it and what bothers me most. Then give me a step-by-step order with rough times, plus simple rules for deciding what to keep, donate, sell or bin.

Troubleshoot a household problem safely

For a machine that's acting up, before you pay for a call-out.

My [appliance and model, if I know it] is [problem, e.g. not draining / showing error code X]. Walk me through safe checks I can do myself, easiest first. Tell me clearly which signs mean I should stop and call a professional, and remind me what to look up in the manual.
Heads-up

Anything involving gas, mains electricity or a smell of burning is a job for a professional, whatever the chat says. Use AI to describe the problem well when you call.

A moving checklist with the admin people forget

Turns a move into weekly chunks instead of one panicked fortnight.

I'm moving from [place] to [place] on [date]. Make a checklist by week, from 8 weeks before to the first week after. Include the admin people forget, like address changes, contracts to cancel or move, and meter readings. Mark anything with a legal deadline and flag what depends on local rules so I can check it.

A fair chore rota for a shared flat

Takes the arguing out of who cleaned the bathroom last.

I share a flat with [number] people. Here are our chores and how often each needs doing: [list]. Make a fair rota that rotates weekly and works around [e.g. one person works nights]. Then write a short, friendly message I can send to the flat group to suggest it.

Food

Dinner from what's in the fridge

The classic, and still one of the most useful.

Here's what's in my fridge and cupboards: [list, or attach a photo]. Give me 3 dinners I can make in under 30 minutes using mostly these. For each: ingredients, short steps, and anything I'd need to buy. I don't eat [dislikes or dietary choices].

A week of dinners and one shopping list

Plan once on Sunday, shop once, stop deciding at 7pm.

Plan 5 dinners for this week for [number] people. Budget about [€ amount] in total, at most [minutes] of cooking on weekdays, and we like [likes] but avoid [dislikes]. Reuse ingredients so little goes to waste. End with one shopping list grouped by supermarket section.

Cook once, eat all week

For people who'd rather spend two hours on Sunday than thirty minutes every night.

I want to cook for [hours] on Sunday and have lunches for the week. Give me 2 dishes that keep well in the fridge, the order to cook them in so nothing sits waiting, and how to store and reheat each one. Note how many days each will keep.

Check storage times against your country's official food safety advice. AI can be off on these.

Scale or adapt a recipe

For halving a recipe, feeding a crowd or cooking without an oven.

Here's a recipe for [servings]: [paste or attach]. Rewrite it for [new number] servings and [change, e.g. no oven / vegetarian / no eggs]. Give all amounts in grams and millilitres, and tell me which steps or cooking times change when the quantity changes.

One menu for guests with different needs

So everyone eats the same main dish and you're not cooking four meals.

I'm cooking for [number] guests on [day]. What they've told me they need: [e.g. one vegetarian, one avoids nuts, one doesn't eat pork]. Suggest a menu where everyone can eat the same main dish, plus a prep timeline so I'm not stuck in the kitchen when people arrive.

For allergies, always check ingredient labels yourself and confirm with the guest.

Money admin

General information, not financial advice. For debt, tax or investment decisions, talk to a qualified adviser or a free advice service in your country.

See where the money actually goes

A clear monthly picture in ten minutes, without a spreadsheet.

Here are my monthly income and costs, without any account numbers: [list]. Sort the costs into fixed, flexible and occasional, show totals in a table, and point out the 3 places where small changes would make the biggest difference. Ask me before assuming anything is unnecessary.

A subscription clear-out

Small monthly charges add up quietly. For a full version, see The 20-minute money-leak audit.

Here are my subscriptions and what each costs per month: [list]. Ask me how often I really use each one. Then sort them into keep, downgrade, pause or cancel, and show what I'd save per year if I follow your suggestions.

Compare two contracts properly

For phone, internet or energy offers where the cheap headline price hides the real cost.

Compare these two [phone / internet / energy] offers over the full contract term: [paste both]. Include setup fees, price changes after the first year and cancellation terms. Show the total cost of each in a table and list what I should double-check in the small print.

Plan a savings goal

Turns "I should save for that" into a monthly number.

I want to save [amount] for [goal] by [date]. After fixed costs I usually have about [amount] left each month. Show what I'd need to set aside monthly, 3 realistic ways to find that money, and what to do if I miss a month.

Split a shared cost fairly

For rent, a holiday house or a group present, before it gets awkward.

Help me split [shared cost] of [amount] between [number] people. Here's what's different between us: [e.g. room sizes / nights stayed / who joined late]. Show 2 fair ways to split it in a table, the amount per person for each, and a short, neutral message explaining the options to the group.

Letters and forms

AI is good at explaining and drafting, but it can misread official wording. For anything with legal consequences, check with the organisation that sent it or get proper advice.

Decode a confusing letter

The prompt I'd teach anyone first. Works on bills, notices and official letters.

I'm attaching a letter, with my personal numbers covered. Tell me in plain words: who sent it, what they want from me, the deadline, what happens if I ignore it, and what my options are. If anything is unclear or could mean two things, say so.

A letter in a language you don't read well

For anyone living abroad, or helping a parent who is.

This letter is in [language], which I don't read well: [paste or attach]. Translate it into plain English, then tell me what I need to do and by when. Flag any official terms that have a specific legal meaning, so I can check them.

Understand a form before you fill it in

Explains each field without you sharing the answers.

I need to fill in [form name] from [organisation]. I'll paste the questions, not my answers. Explain what each field is asking for in plain words, which documents I'll need to hand, and which questions are easy to get wrong. Tell me where to check if you're unsure.

A complaint that gets taken seriously

Polite, factual and hard to ignore.

Help me write a complaint to [company] about [problem]. What happened: [dates, order details, what I've already tried]. I want [refund / replacement / repair]. Keep it under 200 words, polite but firm, and give them a clear date to reply by.

Cancel a contract cleanly

Short, formal and with proof.

Write a cancellation letter for my [contract type] with [company]. I want to cancel at the earliest possible date and get the end date confirmed in writing. Leave a gap for my customer number. Keep it short and formal, and tell me how to send it so I have proof it arrived.

Push back on a decision you think is wrong

For a fine, a refusal or a bill you want to dispute.

I received [a fine / a refusal / a bill] that I think is wrong because [reason]. Explain the options usually available to dispute something like this, what evidence I should collect, and draft a calm, factual first letter. Tell me clearly when I should get professional or legal advice instead.

Health admin

These prompts are for the paperwork around health, not for medical advice. AI can't examine you. For symptoms, talk to a doctor or pharmacist, and in an emergency call your local emergency number.

Prepare for an appointment

So you don't walk out and remember the question you meant to ask.

I have a [type of] appointment on [date]. Here are my notes in my own words: [notes]. Turn them into one page I can read from: what's happening, since when, what I've tried, and the questions I want to ask. Don't diagnose anything or suggest treatments.

Make sense of an insurance letter or bill

For statements and bills that seem written to confuse.

Here's a letter from my [health insurer / clinic], with my policy and patient numbers removed: [paste or attach]. Explain in plain words what it says, what I owe or need to do, and by when. List the questions I should ask them if something doesn't add up.

Track a family member's appointments

Useful when you're the one keeping track for a child or a parent.

Make a simple table to track [family member]'s appointments, check-ups and repeat prescriptions. Columns: date, what it's for, who's going, what to bring, and when to book the next one. Add 3 example rows so I can see how to fill it in.

Work out who to contact

For when you know you need help but not which door to knock on.

I live in [country] and need help with [non-urgent issue, e.g. getting a copy of my vaccination record / a repeat prescription while travelling]. Explain the usual options, such as GP, pharmacy or helpline, and what each one handles. Tell me which official website to check, since rules differ by place.

Travel

Tip

AI doesn't reliably know today's opening hours, prices, timetables or entry rules. Use it to plan, then confirm the details on official sites before you book or travel.

A city break that isn't a march

Groups sights by area so you're not crossing the city four times a day.

Plan 3 days in [city] for [who's going], who like [interests] and dislike [crowds / early starts / museums]. Group sights that are near each other, keep one afternoon free, and add a rainy-day backup. Tell me what I should book in advance.

A packing list that fits the bag

Especially good for hand-luggage-only trips.

Make a packing list for [number] days in [place] in [month] for [activities]. I'm taking only [hand luggage / a backpack]. Group it by category, mark the things people often forget, and say what I could easily buy there instead of carrying.

Train, bus, plane or car?

A side-by-side view before you start comparing booking sites.

I need to get from [A] to [B] around [date]. Compare train, bus, plane and car in a table: rough door-to-door time, comfort, hassle and what usually affects the price. Then tell me which official sites to check for real prices and times.

A trip budget that adds up

Before you book, not after.

Help me budget a [number]-day trip to [place] for [number] people with [€ amount] in total. Split it across transport, accommodation, food, activities and a buffer. Ask me what matters most to us before suggesting where to save.

The phrases you'll actually use

Fifteen phrases beat a phrasebook you never open.

I'm visiting [country] and don't speak [language]. Give me the 15 phrases I'll really use, like greetings, ordering, directions, paying and saying sorry, with simple pronunciation. Then add 5 local customs a polite visitor should know.

Family and friends

The message you've been avoiding

For saying no, saying sorry or saying something hard.

I need to tell [person, and how I know them] that [hard thing]. Write 3 versions under 80 words each: gentle, direct, and in between. Avoid blaming language, and suggest one thing I could say if they react badly.

A birthday on a budget

From guest list to timeline in one go.

Plan a birthday for [who, their age and interests] for about [number] guests with a budget of [€ amount]. Give me a timeline for the day, a shopping list, and 3 activity ideas that don't need a big space.

Tech help for a relative

Instructions you can send instead of explaining it on the phone five times.

My [relative] wants to start using [app or device]. Write step-by-step instructions for a complete beginner: one action per step, no jargon, and what they should see on screen after each step. Add a short "if you get stuck" section at the end.

Rainy-day ideas for kids

When everyone is bored by 10am.

Give me 10 indoor activities for [number] children aged [ages] on a rainy day, using things most homes already have. Mix quiet and active ones, mark which need an adult, and put the ones with the least clearing up first.

A note that says what you mean

Thank-you notes, sympathy cards and congratulations that don't sound like a greeting card.

Help me write a short [thank-you / sympathy / congratulations] note to [person and relationship] about [occasion]. Here's what I want to say, in my own messy words: [notes]. Keep my meaning and my details, make it warm and natural, and keep it under [number] words.

Learning

Learn a skill in 30 days

A plan that fits real life, not an ideal week.

I want to learn [skill] in 30 days with [minutes] a day. Ask me 3 questions about my level and goal first. Then give me a week-by-week plan with one small win each week and a simple way to tell if it's working.

Understand it, then prove it

Explanation plus a quick check, so you know it stuck.

Explain [topic] to me as if I'm smart but completely new to it. Use one everyday example. Then ask me 3 questions to check I understood, one at a time, and correct me gently.

Practise a language without the fear

Speaking practice with a partner who never gets impatient.

I'm learning [language] at [level]. Play a friendly [shopkeeper / waiter / neighbour] and chat with me in simple [language]. After each of my replies, correct my mistakes briefly in English, then carry on the conversation.

Remember what you read

Makes you explain it first, which is what makes it stick.

I just read [chapter or article; paste it or name it]. Ask me to explain the main idea in my own words first. Then tell me what I got right, what I missed, and one way I could use it this week.

Help with homework without doing it

For parents who've forgotten long division.

My child is [age] and stuck on [homework topic]. Explain it to me first in simple terms so I can help. Then give me 3 hints I can offer, in order, that guide them without giving away the answer.

Understand a news story from all sides

For when everyone has an opinion and you just want the facts.

Explain [news topic] neutrally: what happened, the main arguments on each side in their strongest form, and what's still unknown. Say clearly if your information might be out of date, and suggest the kinds of reliable sources I should read for updates.

Decisions

Choose between two options

The AI asks first, so the answer is about your life, not a generic pros and cons list.

I'm deciding between [option A] and [option B]. Ask me 5 questions, one at a time, about what matters to me. Then tell me which option fits my answers better, why, and what would change your mind.

Find the flaws before they find you

A "pre-mortem": imagine it failed, then work out why.

Here's my plan: [plan]. Imagine it's six months from now and it went badly. Give me the 5 most likely reasons, the early warning sign for each, and the cheapest way to test the riskiest one first.

Score your options

For choices with lots of factors, like which flat to rent.

Help me compare [options] for [decision]. First ask me which 4 or 5 things matter most and how much each one matters. Then score each option in a table and show the totals. Remind me the scores are only as good as my inputs.

Think twice before a big purchase

Ten minutes now can save a lot of regret.

I'm thinking of buying [item] for about [€ price]. Ask me what I'll use it for. Then tell me what to look for, what isn't worth paying extra for, cheaper options like second-hand, renting or borrowing, and what to ask before I buy.

Make them better

  • Add your constraints. Budget, time, who's involved, what you've already tried. These change the answer more than anything else.
  • Ask for a format. A table, a checklist or "under 100 words" makes the answer easier to use straight away.
  • Save the ones you reuse. Keep your favourites in a notes app, or set up a project in your AI app just for home admin, so the context stays in one place.
  • Say what's wrong, not just "again". "Too expensive, and we don't have an oven" gets a better second try than "try again".

Where AI falls short in everyday life

AI is good at explaining, drafting, planning and organising. It's weaker on anything current or local: today's prices, opening hours, timetables, and rules that changed recently or differ by country. It can also sound certain when it's guessing. Treat its answers on money, law, health and tax as a well-organised starting point, then confirm with the official source or a professional before you act. And keep the personal parts human: AI can help you write the hard message, but you should be the one who decides to send it.

Questions people ask

Which AI app should I use for these?

Any of the big ones works: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot. I use Claude most, especially for writing and long letters. If you live in Google apps, Gemini is worth trying first. Pick your AI by job compares them.

Is it safe to upload a photo of a letter?

It's reasonable if you cover personal numbers first, such as customer, tax, insurance and bank details. Check your app's privacy settings to see whether chats are used for training, and avoid sharing other people's private information without their consent.

Can I trust what it says about rules and deadlines?

Use it to understand and plan, not as the final word. AI can be wrong or out of date, and rules differ by country. Confirm deadlines and legal points with the organisation or an official website.

Do I need a paid plan?

No. Every prompt here works on the free plans of the main AI apps. Paid plans mostly give you more use per day and extra features, which you don't need to start.

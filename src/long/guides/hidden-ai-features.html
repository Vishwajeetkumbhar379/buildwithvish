Most people use AI like a search box: type a question, read the answer, close the tab. The apps can do far more, but the useful features sit behind small icons and settings pages that nobody opens. This page covers 15 of them across ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, checked in October 2026. For each one you'll get what it does, where to find it in each app, and a prompt to try. You don't need to learn all 15. Read the list, pick the three that fit your week, and try them today. That takes about 30 minutes.

In short
  • The biggest quick wins are free: editing an earlier message instead of starting over, custom instructions, and temporary chats.
  • Projects (or notebooks in Gemini) stop you re-explaining the same background in every chat.
  • Voice, camera and photos make AI useful away from your desk: a confusing form, a broken tap, a menu in another language.
  • Deep research, scheduled tasks and connectors turn the assistant from something you ask into something that does work for you.
  • Saved assistants are changing: ChatGPT's custom GPTs and Gemini's Gems are being replaced in late 2026.
The plan
  1. Fix the basics Edit and retry, custom instructions, temporary chats, memory. 10 min
  2. Stop repeating yourself One Project for something ongoing. 5 min
  3. Go beyond typing Try voice, then a photo of something confusing. 5 min
  4. Hand over a bigger job One deep research report or one scheduled task. 10 min

Get better answers from the same chat

1. Edit your earlier message instead of starting again

When an answer goes wrong, most people type "no, I meant…" underneath. The better move is to go back and edit the message that caused it. The AI rewrites its answer from that point, and the bad turn no longer clutters the conversation. You can usually flick between the old and new versions to compare.

Where: in Gemini, click "Edit text" next to your message, change it and click "Update". Gemini can also show other drafts of its latest answer. In ChatGPT and Claude, hover over a message you sent and look for the edit (pencil) icon. Since June 2026, ChatGPT lets you edit messages that include attachments too.

Tip

Edit when the answer went wrong because of what you asked. Follow up when the answer is mostly right and needs polishing.

2. Temporary chats for one-off questions

A temporary chat isn't saved to your history and doesn't feed your memory. Use it for a question you don't want shaping future answers, or anything a bit private.

Where: ChatGPT: open a new chat and select "Temporary". Claude: in a new chat outside a Project, click the ghost icon to start an incognito chat. Gemini: on the web, click "Temporary chat" next to "New chat". Each company still keeps these chats for a short time for safety: up to 30 days at OpenAI and Anthropic, and 72 hours at Google.

3. Custom instructions: tell it once

Custom instructions are a note the assistant reads before every chat: who you are, where you live, how you like answers. It's the single setting that most improves everyday answers, and it takes five minutes.

Where: ChatGPT: Settings, Personalization, then switch on "Enable customization". Claude: Settings, General, "Instructions for Claude". Gemini: Settings & help, Personal Intelligence, "Instructions for Gemini", then "Add".

About me: I'm [job or situation] in [country]. Use [currency] and local rules unless I say otherwise.
How I like answers:
- Answer first, then explain. Short unless I ask for detail.
- Plain British English. Explain jargon the first time.
- Tables when comparing options.
- If my request is unclear, ask me up to three questions first.
- Say when you're not sure. Never invent facts, sources or prices.

4. Memory you can actually read

All three can remember facts about you between chats. Most people never check what's stored, so an old job title or a one-off request keeps quietly shaping answers.

Where: ChatGPT: Settings, Personalization, Memory. Claude: Settings, then Memory. Gemini: Settings & help, Personal Intelligence, then the Memory switch (it needs "Keep Activity" on). You can also just ask.

List everything you currently remember about me, grouped into work, home and preferences.
Mark anything that looks out of date, anything you're unsure about, and anything I might not want stored. Then tell me how to remove items I choose.

Stop repeating yourself

5. Projects (notebooks in Gemini)

A Project is a workspace with its own files and instructions. Every chat inside it can see them. Use one for each ongoing thing: a job search, a renovation, a course, a side business.

Where: Claude: "Projects" in the sidebar, then "+ New Project" (Free accounts get up to five). ChatGPT: "New project" in the sidebar (5 files per Project on Free). Gemini: open the sidebar and choose "New notebook" under Notebooks. Gemini notebooks sync with Gemini Notebook, the research tool formerly called NotebookLM. The full walkthrough is Projects in Claude and ChatGPT, explained.

This workspace is for [goal, e.g. finding a marketing job in Germany by March].
The files here are [what you uploaded, e.g. my CV and two job ads I like].
In every chat: check the files before answering and say which one you used, keep a running list of decisions and open questions, and ask me before assuming anything that isn't in the files.

6. Search your past chats

"What did we decide about the venue last month?" Instead of scrolling, ask. Claude's chat search (paid plans) looks through past conversations when you ask it to. ChatGPT can reference your chat history if that memory option is on. Gemini's Memory learns from past chats, and you can ask it "Did you use any info from past chats?" to see what it drew on.

Search our past chats for anything about [topic, e.g. the kitchen quote from the builder].
Tell me what we decided, when, and what was still open. Quote the key line from the original chat so I can check it.

7. Saved assistants for tasks you repeat

If you paste the same long prompt every week, save it. This is the feature that's changing most right now.

  • Claude Skills: saved instructions, sometimes with templates, that Claude loads when a task needs them. Find them under Customize, then Skills. They need file creation switched on. See Turn a task you repeat into a Claude Skill.
  • Gemini skills replacing Gems: Google's skills help page says Gems will turn into skills automatically, and TechCrunch reported the switch is set for 17 November 2026. Skills can be created with Gemini's help, from a template or by hand.
  • ChatGPT plugins replacing GPTs: According to OpenAI's release notes and retirement FAQ, OpenAI plans to stop new custom GPTs on 26 October 2026, and existing ones stop working on 11 December 2026. Don't build a new GPT now. Use a Project, or a plugin when you need a connected workflow.

Use more than text

8. Voice conversations

Talking is faster than typing and better for thinking out loud. Practise a hard conversation, rehearse a presentation, or talk through tomorrow on a walk.

Where: ChatGPT: the Voice icon in the message bar. Claude: the sound-wave icon in the message box. Gemini: the Live button in the mobile app. Free plans include voice with limits.

I need to [hard conversation, e.g. ask my manager for a deadline extension]. Play [the other person]. Ask one question at a time and push back the way they realistically might. Keep your replies short. After five exchanges, tell me what I did well and the one thing to change.

9. Share your camera or screen while you talk

On your phone, you can point the camera at something and talk about it: a washing machine error, a plant, a confusing ticket machine. Or share your screen while you work through a form.

Where: Gemini Live on Android: tap "Turn on video streaming" for the camera or "Turn on screen sharing". ChatGPT on iPhone and Android: in a voice chat, the camera button shares live video, and the more-options menu has "Share Screen" (OpenAI's voice FAQ says this needs the Advanced voice option). I couldn't find live camera sharing in Claude's help pages when I checked, but you can send it photos.

Heads-up

Close banking apps and private messages before sharing your screen. Whatever is visible may be sent.

10. Photos and files: the confusing-letter trick

Every assistant can read photos, PDFs, Word files and spreadsheets. The most useful everyday version: photograph an official letter, a form or a menu in another language and ask what it means. Cover account and ID numbers first.

I've attached a photo of [a letter / a form / a contract page]. Tell me:
1. Who it's from and what it's about, in one sentence.
2. What it wants me to do, and by when. Quote the exact deadline.
3. What happens if I ignore it.
4. Anything unusual worth asking about.
If any part is hard to read, tell me rather than guessing.

This is general help, not legal or financial advice. For anything with a legal deadline or real money at stake, check with the sender or a qualified professional.

Let it do bigger jobs

11. Deep research

Deep research runs many web searches, reads the results and writes a cited report. It takes minutes, not seconds. Use it for questions you'd otherwise spend an afternoon searching.

Where: ChatGPT: type /deepresearch or pick Deep research from the + menu (Free gets a small allowance). Gemini: Deep Research is listed on the free plan. Claude: click +, then "Research" (paid plans).

Research: [question, e.g. the cheapest reliable ways to send money from Germany to India each month].
Context: [who you are and what you'll use this for].
Start with a five-bullet answer, then a comparison table, then what changed in the last 12 months. Prefer official sources, date every source, and mark where sources disagree.

12. Build a mini tool

Ask for a small tool and you get one you can use straight away: a calculator, a tracker, a checklist you can tick. For many people this is the moment AI stops feeling like a chatbot.

Where: Claude makes these as artifacts in a window next to the chat, on every plan. Gemini has Canvas, listed on its free plan. ChatGPT's Work mode (paid) can build simple websites, which OpenAI calls Sites.

Build me a simple tool that [does one thing, e.g. splits rent fairly between three flatmates based on room size in square metres]. Make it work on my phone and fit on one screen. Ask me up to three questions first if you need to, then explain in two lines how to use it.

13. Real files you can download

Instead of copying a table out of the chat, ask for the actual file: an Excel sheet with working formulas, a PowerPoint deck, a Word document.

Where: Claude: switch on "Code execution and file creation" under Settings, then Capabilities (all plans), then ask. ChatGPT: Work mode on paid plans builds documents, spreadsheets and presentations.

Create an Excel file to track [what, e.g. my job applications]. Columns: [list them]. Add a drop-down for status, a second sheet that counts applications by status with formulas, and five example rows. Explain in three lines how to add my own.

14. Scheduled tasks

A scheduled task runs a prompt on a timetable: a Monday briefing, a daily reminder, a weekly price check.

Where: ChatGPT: open "Scheduled" and ask in plain words (Free allows 3 active tasks). Claude: open "Scheduled", then "New task" (paid plans). Gemini: describe the schedule in your prompt, then manage it under Settings & help, "Scheduled actions" (up to 10 active, with Keep Activity on; still rolling out to personal accounts, according to Google's scheduled actions help page).

Every Monday at 7:30, send me a short briefing: the weather for [city] this week with rainy days flagged, three news items about [topic] from the past week with a one-line "why it matters" and the source, and a reminder to [task]. Under 200 words, readable on a phone.

15. Connectors: let it see your real information

Connectors let the assistant read your calendar, email or files, so answers use your actual week instead of guesses.

Where: Claude: Customize, then Connectors. ChatGPT: Settings, then Plugins. Gemini: Settings & help, then Connected Apps. Connect one app you'd really use, not everything at once. Connectors and MCP, explained simply covers what access you're giving.

Look at my calendar for this week. For each meeting, tell me in one line what I should prepare. Flag clashes and days with no breaks, and suggest two blocks of focus time. Don't change, accept or send anything.

What these features can't do

  • Memory isn't a filing cabinet. It stores selected details, not everything, and can store the wrong thing. Check it now and then.
  • Deep research can still be wrong. More sources doesn't mean better sources. Open the ones behind any number you'll repeat.
  • Scheduled tasks run without you. If the prompt is vague, you'll get a vague briefing every week. Test the prompt by hand first.
  • Connectors widen what can go wrong. A connected assistant can read more of your life, and some can act on it. Start with read-only jobs and tell it not to send or change anything.
  • Features vary by plan and country. Some arrive in the US first, and work accounts may have features switched off by an admin.

When it gets stuck

  • "I don't see the feature." Check you're on the right plan, update the app, and search the help centre for the feature name. Some features are web-only or mobile-only.
  • "Memory keeps bringing up old stuff." Open the memory settings and delete the outdated item, or switch memory off for a while.
  • "Gemini won't use my Gmail or schedule anything." Both need "Keep Activity" on. Check Settings & help, then Activity.
  • "The mini tool doesn't work properly." Describe exactly what happens: "When I click Add, nothing appears." Ask it to fix that one thing.
  • "The scheduled task never arrived." Check notifications are allowed for the app, and check the task is still active. Gemini can pause inactive scheduled actions.
  • "My GPT or Gem disappeared." They're being retired in late 2026. Copy your instructions into a Project, a skill or a plugin.

Questions people ask

Which of these should I try first?

Custom instructions, then editing your messages. Both are free, take minutes and improve every answer after that.

Do I need a paid plan for these?

Most of them work on free plans with limits. The main paid-only ones are Claude's Research, chat search and scheduled tasks, and ChatGPT's Work mode. Check the plan pages before you pay.

Are temporary chats completely private?

No. They aren't saved to your history or memory, but each company keeps them for a short time for safety. Don't paste passwords, ID numbers or bank details into any chat. More in the privacy settings guide.

What's the difference between a Project and a skill?

A Project holds the files and instructions for one ongoing piece of work. A skill holds how to do one type of task, and can be used in any chat.

Where can I learn each app properly?

Start with the full guide for your main tool: Claude from zero, ChatGPT from zero or Gemini for people who live in Google.

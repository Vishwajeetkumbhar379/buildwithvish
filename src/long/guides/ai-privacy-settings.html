You don't need to be paranoid about AI. You do need to know what you've agreed to, because the defaults weren't chosen with you in mind. This page walks you through seven privacy settings in ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, with Microsoft Copilot where I could check it, using the exact paths as they were on 6 October 2026. For each setting you'll learn what it does, what the trade-off is, and where to click. At the end there's a list of things never to paste into any AI. It takes about 20 minutes for your main app, and five for each extra one. Do it once, then check again every few months.

In short
  • On personal plans, your chats may be used to train future models. ChatGPT and Gemini have this on by default with an off switch, Claude asks you to choose, and Copilot has its own training switches. Decide on purpose.
  • Temporary or incognito chats aren't saved to your history or memory, but each company still keeps them for a short time.
  • Check what memory has stored, delete old chats with personal documents, and download a copy of your data if you want one.
  • Disconnect apps you don't use, and delete shared links you've forgotten about.
  • No setting makes it safe to paste passwords, ID numbers, bank details or other people's private information.

Before you start

Make a list of the AI apps you've signed up to, including ones you tried once. Each has its own settings, and the account you forgot about is often the one holding an old upload.

Then check which kind of account each one is. A personal account (Free, Go, Plus, Pro, Max, Google AI Pro and so on) is covered by the settings below. A work account provided by your employer usually has different, stronger defaults, and your company's admin controls many settings. For example, OpenAI says it doesn't train on Business data, and Google says chats in qualifying Workspace accounts aren't reviewed by humans or used to improve its AI models. If you have a work account, use it for work, and ask your IT team what's allowed.

If you live in the EU, you also have legal rights over your personal data under the GDPR, such as asking for a copy or for deletion. Each company's privacy policy explains how to use them.

1. Model training: decide on purpose

What it is: whether your conversations can be used to improve the company's future AI models. The trade-off: leaving it on helps the models get better; switching it off means your new chats aren't used for that. Your chat history stays either way. Turning it off doesn't undo training that already happened.

AppWhere (October 2026)Good to know
ChatGPTSettings, Data controls, "Improve the model for everyone"On by default for personal plans
ClaudeSettings, Privacy, the model-improvement toggleIf on, de-identified data from new chats can be kept up to 5 years for training
GeminiSettings & help, Activity, "Keep Activity"See the heads-up below: this switch controls more than training
CopilotOn copilot.com: profile, Privacy, "Training on conversation activity" and "Training on voice conversations"Separate switches for text and voice
Heads-up

Thumbs up or down counts. OpenAI says a conversation you give feedback on may be used for training even if you've opted out. Anthropic says feedback-linked conversations can be kept for 5 years. Don't rate a sensitive chat.

Gemini works differently

In Gemini, one setting called "Keep Activity" does several jobs. With it on, your chats are saved to your account and help improve Google's services, including its AI models, and a subset of chats is read by trained human reviewers. Google says reviewed chats are disconnected from your account first and can be kept for up to three years. With it off, chats are kept for up to 72 hours and aren't used for training unless you send feedback. But turning it off also stops memory, Connected Apps on the web and scheduled actions. So you're choosing between features and privacy. If you keep it on, the simple rule is: don't put anything in Gemini you wouldn't want a reviewer to read. Details are on Google's Gemini Apps Privacy Hub.

My take

If you'll ever paste work documents or anything personal into a personal account, I'd switch training off. If you only use AI for recipes and trip plans, leaving it on is a reasonable choice. Either way, make the decision yourself rather than by default.

2. Temporary chats: know where they are

What it is: a chat that isn't saved to your history, doesn't create memories and isn't used for training. Use it for a one-off sensitive question, or anything you don't want shaping future answers. The catch: each company still keeps a copy for a short time for safety.

AppWhereKept for
ChatGPTOpen a new chat and select "Temporary"Up to 30 days
ClaudeNew chat outside a Project, then the ghost icon ("Incognito chat")30 days
GeminiOn the web, "Temporary chat" next to "New chat"Up to 72 hours

Temporary doesn't mean secret. It's still sent to the company's servers. Treat it as "won't clutter my account", not "nobody can ever see it".

3. Memory: see what it remembers

What it is: facts the assistant keeps about you between chats, such as your job, your city or your preferences. The trade-off: it saves you repeating yourself, but old or private details can creep into answers, and it's more information about you held in one place.

AppWhereGood to know
ChatGPTSettings, Personalization, MemoryTwo kinds: saved memories and reference chat history. You can switch each off and delete items.
ClaudeSettings, Memory"Generate memory from chats" switch, plus a separate opt-in for sensitive topics. Projects keep their own memory.
GeminiSettings & help, Personal Intelligence, MemoryNeeds Keep Activity on. Not used in Gems or Live chats.
CopilotProfile, Memory, "Personalization and memory"Also a separate "Microsoft usage data" switch in the same place

The quickest audit is to ask the assistant directly, then delete what you don't want in the settings.

List everything you currently remember about me, grouped into: work, home and family, health or money, and preferences.
Mark anything that's out of date, anything sensitive, and anything I might not want stored.
Then tell me exactly where in this app's settings I can delete each item. If you're not sure where a setting is, say so.
Tip

If you keep memory on, leave out the sensitive stuff. In Claude, I'd keep the sensitive-topics option off. In any app, if you see a health or money detail in memory, delete it.

4. Chat history: delete what you don't need

What it is: your saved conversations. Old chats where you pasted a contract, a bank statement or a CV are worth deleting once you're done. The catch: deleting isn't instant everywhere. It usually disappears from your account at once and from the company's systems a little later.

AppHow to deleteWhat happens
ChatGPTThe ••• menu next to a chat, then Delete. All at once: Settings, Data controls, "Delete all chats".Permanently deleted within 30 days. Files in Library must be deleted separately.
ClaudeHover over a chat, the three dots, then Delete. Many: "Select", then "Select all".Removed from Anthropic's back-end systems within 30 days
GeminiSettings & help, Activity, then Delete: last hour, last day, all time or a custom rangeAuto-delete defaults to 18 months; you can choose 3 or 36 months, or none

Heads-up

Archiving is not deleting. In ChatGPT an archived chat is only hidden and stays stored until you delete it. And because ChatGPT saves uploads to your Library, delete sensitive files there too.

While you're in Gemini's Activity page, set auto-delete to the shortest period you're comfortable with. Click "Deleting activity older than…" and pick 3 months if you don't need older chats.

5. Data export: get a copy

What it is: a download of your conversations and account data. Useful before you delete an account, switch tools, or just to see what's stored.

  • ChatGPT: Settings, Data controls, "Export data". You get a ZIP file by email, and the link expires after 24 hours.
  • Claude: click your initials, Settings, Privacy, "Export data". The email link expires 24 hours after it arrives, and export works on the web and desktop app, not mobile. See Anthropic's export help page.
  • Gemini: go to takeout.google.com, click "Deselect all", then tick "Gemini" (for your Gems) and, under "My Activity", "Gemini Apps" (for chats, uploads and generated media). Google emails a link that expires after 7 days. Steps are on Google's download help page.

Store the download somewhere safe. It contains everything you ever typed, so treat it like a bank statement.

6. Connected apps: remove access you don't use

What it is: permissions you gave the assistant to read (and sometimes act in) your email, calendar, files or other apps. The risk: each connection widens what the assistant can see. A connection you set up for one experiment months ago is still there.

AppWhere
ChatGPTSettings, Plugins, select the app, then Disconnect (from the account's ••• menu or the Connection option)
ClaudeCustomize, then Connectors
GeminiSettings & help, Connected Apps (or Personal Intelligence, then Connected Apps), and switch off the ones you don't use

Also check from the other side. Your Google or Microsoft account has its own list of third-party apps with access to it. Remove any AI tool there you no longer use. Disconnecting stops future access; it doesn't delete what the assistant already saw in past chats. For what each kind of access means, see Connectors and MCP, explained simply.

7. Shared links: kill the old ones

What it is: if you've ever shared a chat by link, anyone with that link can usually read it, no account needed. Links get forwarded, pasted into documents and forgotten.

  • ChatGPT: Settings, Data controls, then "Manage" next to Shared links. Delete one or all. Deleting the original chat also kills its link. OpenAI says shared links aren't designed to appear in search results, but anyone with the URL can open them.
  • Claude: Settings, Privacy, then the Shared chats section. A link shares a snapshot as of when you shared it; later messages stay private unless you update it. See Anthropic's page on public links.
  • Gemini: Settings & help, "Your public links", then the delete icon next to each, or "Delete all links". Deleting a link doesn't delete the chat from your activity, and can't remove copies other people saved. See Google's sharing help page.

What I never paste into any AI

Settings reduce risk. They don't remove it. Your chats still travel to a company's servers, can be kept for safety reviews, and can be exposed if your account is compromised. So some things stay out entirely:

  • Passwords, PINs and one-time codes. No AI needs them.
  • Full card numbers, bank account numbers and online banking screenshots.
  • ID, passport, tax and social security numbers.
  • Other people's private information without their OK: a friend's health, a colleague's salary, a client's personal details.
  • Company documents your employer hasn't approved for AI use, especially in a personal account.
  • Medical records with your name and identifiers on them. If you want help understanding a letter, cover the personal details first.
Risky

A photo of your whole tax letter with your name, address and tax ID visible, uploaded to a personal account with training switched on.

Better

The same letter with name, address and numbers covered, in a temporary chat, asking "What does this letter want me to do, and by when?"

Before uploading a document, you can ask the AI to help you prepare it without the AI seeing it first: describe the document type and ask what to remove.

I want help understanding a [type of document, e.g. letter from my health insurer about a payment]. I haven't uploaded it yet.
List the kinds of personal details on a document like this that I should cover or remove before sharing it with an AI, and which details you'd still need to explain it properly. Keep it to a short checklist.

Ask the AI to walk you through it

Settings pages change. The assistant itself can often point you to the current layout, though it can be out of date about its own app, so treat its answer as a starting point and check the help centre if a step doesn't match.

Walk me through the privacy and data settings in this app, one at a time: model training, temporary chats, memory, deleting history, data export, connected apps and shared links.
For each one, tell me what it does, where to find it, and the trade-off of switching it on or off.
If you're not sure where a setting is in the current version of the app, say so and tell me what to search for in the help centre.

ChatGPT also has a Privacy Center that gathers these explanations in one place. On the web it's in the account menu, under Help, then "Privacy center". It explains the settings; you still change them in Settings.

What these settings can't do

  • They don't stop safety retention. Every company keeps some data for a period to detect abuse, even with training off or in temporary chats. Anthropic, for example, says chats flagged by its safety systems can be kept for up to 2 years.
  • They don't undo the past. Switching training off affects future chats. Deleting a chat doesn't remove it from anyone's copy of a shared link they saved.
  • They don't cover your employer's tools. A work account follows your company's rules and admin settings.
  • Other apps I couldn't check in detail. Meta's AI has its own help pages, such as one on removing posts, chats and media, but I couldn't confirm the exact menu paths, so I've left them out. For any other app, look for the same seven settings.

When it gets stuck

  • "I can't find the training setting." On a work account, your admin may control it. On a personal account, search the help centre for "model improvement" or "training".
  • "Gemini lost my memory and Connected Apps." You probably turned Keep Activity off. That's the trade-off. Turn it back on only if you want those features.
  • "The export email never came." Check spam, wait a few hours (Google says it can take hours to days), and request it again from the web, not the mobile app.
  • "I deleted a chat but the file is still there." In ChatGPT, uploads live in Library. Delete them there too.
  • "I shared a link months ago and can't remember what was in it." Delete all shared links. It costs nothing, and you can reshare anything you still need.
  • "I pasted something I shouldn't have." Delete the chat, and if it was a password or code, change it straight away. If it was someone else's data at work, tell your manager or data protection contact.

  • Set a reminder to repeat this check in three months.

Questions people ask

If I switch off training, is my data deleted?

No. Your chats stay in your history until you delete them. Switching off training only means new chats aren't used to improve the models.

Is incognito or temporary chat completely private?

No. It isn't saved to your history or memory and isn't used for training, but the company keeps it briefly for safety (up to 30 days at OpenAI and Anthropic, up to 72 hours at Google).

Which assistant is the most private?

It depends more on your settings and plan type than the brand. A work account with business terms, training switched off and nothing sensitive pasted beats any personal account used carelessly. Pick your AI by job covers the other differences.

Should I turn memory off completely?

Not necessarily. Memory is useful for things like your job and preferred answer style. Keep it on if you like it, check it every month or two, and keep sensitive topics out.

Can I use AI for work documents?

Only within your employer's rules. Many companies provide a business account with stronger protections. If yours doesn't, ask before uploading anything confidential, and never use a personal account with training switched on for company data.

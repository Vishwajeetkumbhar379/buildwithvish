ChatGPT is the AI assistant made by OpenAI, and for most people it's the first one they try. You type or talk, and it answers questions, rewrites emails, reads documents, searches the web, makes images and, on paid plans, works through longer jobs like a report or a spreadsheet. This guide is for complete beginners. By the end you'll have it set up properly, know which plan and model you need (often the free one), know where each feature and privacy switch lives, and have more than 60 prompts to copy. Reading takes about 35 minutes, setup 10. Keep ChatGPT open in another tab and try things as you go.

In short
  • Start on the Free plan. It gives you unlimited everyday text chats and web search, plus limited uploads, images, voice and deep research. Pay only when you keep hitting a limit you care about.
  • Spend 10 minutes on setup: write custom instructions, pick a personality, decide on memory and switch off model training if you'd rather your chats weren't used.
  • Most bad answers come from short, vague requests. Say who it's for, what you want, what good looks like, and ask it to ask you questions first.
  • Use Projects for anything ongoing, search for anything recent, deep research for anything you'd otherwise spend an afternoon googling.
  • Check anything that matters. ChatGPT can be fluent and wrong at the same time, especially on numbers, laws and recent events.

What ChatGPT is, and who makes it

ChatGPT is made by OpenAI, an American AI company. Under the hood it runs on large language models: software trained on huge amounts of text so it can read and write much like a well-read person. You don't need special commands. Plain sentences work, in English, German or most other major languages.

Here's where it earns its place:

  • Writing and rewriting. Emails, posts, CVs, complaint letters. Give it a rough draft and a goal.
  • Explaining. A confusing official letter, a chart at work, a new topic. It explains at your level and doesn't mind the tenth follow-up.
  • Thinking things through. Plans, decisions, "what am I missing?" A patient sparring partner at midnight.
  • Looking things up. With web search it finds current information and shows its sources.
  • Making things. Images, spreadsheets, and on paid plans finished documents and presentations.

And where it lets you down: it can state wrong facts with total confidence (people call this "hallucinating"). Its built-in knowledge stops at a training cut-off, so anything recent needs search. It only knows your situation if you tell it. And it isn't a doctor, lawyer or tax adviser, even when it sounds like one.

Where you can use it

You must be at least 13 to use ChatGPT, and under-18s need a parent's or guardian's permission, according to OpenAI's terms. You can try basic chat and web search on the website without an account, but signing up (free) gives you history, settings and the full feature set. Your chats sync across the web, desktop and mobile apps.

Chat, Work and Codex: three modes

Since mid-2026 the ChatGPT apps have three modes, and it helps to know which one you're in.

  • Chat is the classic conversation. This is where you'll spend most of your time.
  • Work is an agent: ChatGPT carries out a multi-step job on its own and hands you a finished result, such as a document, spreadsheet, presentation or simple website. It's on paid plans, with limited access for Free users in the desktop app.
  • Codex is OpenAI's coding agent. Ignore it until you want to build something.

On the desktop app you switch between Chat and Work with a toggle at the top of the page. On web and mobile, Work sits in a dropdown menu. OpenAI explains the difference in its Work and Codex help page.

My take

Use Free properly for two weeks. If you hit the same limit several times a week, or want Work to build documents for you, Plus is the sensible upgrade. Go is a cheaper middle step for more of the basics, but it can show ads and lacks the stronger model. Pro is for people who code or research for hours a day.

Ads on Free and Go

OpenAI started showing ads to Free and Go users in the US in February 2026 and has been adding countries since. Plus and above are ad-free. Ads can be personalised using your chat context and memory. You can turn that personalisation off under Settings, then Ad Controls.

Models: what the picker means

A model is the AI "brain" behind the answers. OpenAI has several, and on paid plans you choose how much thinking you want with the model picker. More thinking means slower but more careful answers. Here's what each plan sees in Chat, according to OpenAI's model help page in October 2026:

Work and Codex use newer models (GPT-6 Sol, GPT-6 Luna and GPT-6.1 Sol, plus GPT-6 Astra for heavier jobs), which OpenAI said in September 2026 weren't yet in Chat. Don't memorise any of this. Names change every few months.

One change matters if you pay. Since 14 September 2026, Plus and Pro no longer switch automatically from Instant to a thinking model, and typing "think harder" won't change the model. So you pick. My rule of thumb:

  • Instant for anything you could answer yourself in a minute.
  • Medium for planning, comparing, calculating or anything you'll act on.
  • High when a mistake would cost you money, time or face.
  • On Free or Go, press Think before sending anything with numbers, logic or several steps.

Set it up in 10 minutes

These steps improve every future answer. Menu labels use the app's American spelling, exactly as you'll see them.

1. Create your account and get the apps (2 minutes)

Go to chatgpt.com and sign up with your email address or one of the sign-in options shown. Install the mobile app from your phone's app store and, if you like, the desktop app from the ChatGPT website. Use the same account everywhere so your chats follow you.

2. Write your custom instructions (4 minutes)

Custom instructions are a note ChatGPT reads before every new chat. On web or desktop, open Settings, then Personalization, and switch on Enable customization. On mobile it's Settings, then Customize ChatGPT. Free and Go allow 1,500 characters, paid plans 5,000. Changes apply to new chats only. Fill in this template and paste it in:

About me: I'm [first name], I live in [country] and work as [job]. I mostly use ChatGPT for [three things, e.g. work emails, learning German, planning trips].

How to answer:
- Lead with the answer, then the reasoning.
- Use plain British English and short paragraphs. Bullet points for steps.
- If my request is unclear or missing something important, ask me up to 3 questions before answering.
- If something depends on my country, assume [country] and say so.
- When you're not sure, say so. Never invent facts, sources, prices or quotes.
- For money, legal or health topics, give general information and tell me when I should ask a professional.
- Keep it under 300 words unless I ask for more.

3. Pick a personality (1 minute)

In the same Personalization area you'll find Base style and tone. The options are Default, Professional, Friendly, Candid, Quirky, Efficient and Cynical. Under Characteristics you can dial four things up or down: Warm, Enthusiastic, Headers & Lists, and Emojis. These change tone only, not what ChatGPT can do.

I'd try Efficient (answer first, short reasoning) or Candid (direct, honest feedback), with fewer emojis. The default style can be a little too agreeable when you want critique.

4. Decide on memory (1 minute)

ChatGPT has two kinds of memory. Saved memories are facts it stores about you, either because you asked or because it judged them useful. Reference chat history lets it draw on your past conversations. Both live under Settings, Personalization, Memory, where you can switch each one off and see or delete what's been saved. Details vary by plan and region, and OpenAI keeps an up-to-date memory FAQ.

Memory saves you repeating your job, country and preferences. The cost is that old assumptions creep into answers. I keep it on and check what's stored once a month.

5. Choose your privacy setting (1 minute)

On personal plans (Free, Go, Plus and Pro), OpenAI can use your conversations to improve its models unless you opt out. To opt out, open Settings, then Data controls, and switch off Improve the model for everyone. Your chat history stays. It's your call. I'd switch it off if you'll ever paste work documents.

6. Learn where Temporary chat is (1 minute)

A temporary chat doesn't appear in your history, doesn't create memories and isn't used for training. Open a new chat and select Temporary, then choose whether it should use your existing custom instructions and memories (Personalized) or not (Unpersonalized). OpenAI may keep a copy for up to 30 days for safety reasons. Use it for one-off sensitive questions.

How to ask so you get good answers

In my years running creator campaigns on the brand side, most bad work traced back to a vague brief. AI is exactly the same. "Write a post about our sale" gets you something bland. Give it the same information you'd give a freelancer and the answer jumps in quality.

A good request covers five things: the goal, who it's for, the background, what good looks like, and the format you want back. Here's a template you can reuse for almost anything:

I need [what you want, e.g. an email to my landlord asking for a repair].

Who it's for: [audience, and what they care about].
Background: [the facts it needs, paste anything relevant].
Good looks like: [tone, length, what must be included, what to avoid].
Format: [e.g. subject line plus email under 150 words].

Before you start, ask me any questions you need answered to do this well. Then give me two versions that differ in tone.
Weak

"Write a message to my landlord about the heating."

Better

"Write a polite but firm email to my landlord. The heating in my flat has been broken for 6 days, I reported it by phone on Monday and nothing happened. I want a repair date by Friday. Under 150 words, friendly tone, no threats. Ask me if anything's missing."

Then follow up, because the first answer is a draft. Short follow-ups do most of the work: "shorter", "more direct", "what would a sceptic say?", "now as a table". I've collected the ones that work best in 12 follow-ups that fix weak AI answers, and the longer version of the brief method is in Brief AI the way you'd brief a pro.

Tip

Paste examples. "Here are two emails I've written that I like. Match this style" beats any description of your style. Same for formats: paste a report you like and say "structure it like this".

The features, one by one

Each feature below has what it does, where to find it, which plans have it, and a prompt to try. Most tools live behind the + button in the message box. On the web you can also type / to pick a tool.

File uploads and Library

Give ChatGPT PDFs, Word documents, spreadsheets, presentations or images and ask about them. Attach a file with the + button in the message box. With spreadsheets it can run code behind the scenes to analyse data and draw charts. Every file you upload or create is saved to your Library in the sidebar. OpenAI lists a limit of 512 MB per file (about 50 MB for spreadsheets, 20 MB for images), and storage from 500 MB on Free to 100 GB on Pro.

I've attached [a contract / a letter / a report]. I'm [who you are] and I need to [decide something / reply / understand my obligations].

1. Summarise it in 5 plain-English bullet points.
2. List every date, deadline, fee and obligation that applies to me, with the section it's in.
3. Flag anything unusual or one-sided, quoting the exact wording.
4. Tell me the 3 questions I should ask before I agree or reply.
Heads-up

Because uploads are saved to Library, deleting a chat doesn't delete the file you attached. If you uploaded something sensitive, open Library, select the file and delete it too.

Web search

ChatGPT searches the web by itself when a question needs current information, and links its sources. To force a search, choose Search from the tools menu, or type / and pick it. It's on every plan, even signed out. Open the sources for anything that matters: OpenAI itself says results can be incomplete, out of date or wrong.

Search the web: [question, e.g. what are the current rules for bringing a dog on a train in Germany]?

Use official or primary sources where possible. Give me:
- the answer in 3 to 5 bullets,
- the date each source was published or updated,
- anything that differs between sources.

If you can't find a reliable answer, say so instead of guessing.

Deep research

Deep research is search on a bigger scale. ChatGPT proposes a research plan, which you can edit, then works through many sources and writes a structured report with citations. It can also use your files and supported connected apps. Type /deepresearch, pick Deep research from the + menu, or choose it in the sidebar. Free gets a small allowance. Reports download as Markdown, Word or PDF.

Research [topic, e.g. the best ways for a small online shop in the EU to take payments in 2026].

Context: [who you are, budget, country, what you'll use the report for].

I want a one-paragraph answer at the top, a comparison table of the main options (cost, effort, pros, cons), what changed in the last 12 months, and a recommendation for my situation with reasoning.

Prefer official sources. Show me your plan before you start.

Projects

A Project is a folder that keeps chats, files and instructions together, so ChatGPT has the context for an ongoing topic every time. Select New project in the sidebar, name it, then add files and instructions. Free projects hold 5 files, Go and Plus 25, Pro and above 40. Choose project-only memory if chats inside should draw on that project alone. Projects can be shared with view or edit access. More in Projects in Claude and ChatGPT, explained.

You're helping me with my job search in [country]. I'm targeting [role] roles at [type of company]. My CV and two cover letters I like are in this project's files.

In every chat in this project:
- tailor everything to the job ad I paste, using only facts from my CV,
- write in plain, confident British English with no buzzwords,
- point out gaps between my CV and the ad, and honest ways to address them.

Memory

In daily use, you can tell ChatGPT to remember something ("remember that I'm vegetarian"), ask what it knows about you, and remove anything outdated in the memory settings. Temporary chats never create memories. Outdated memories cause quietly wrong answers, so check now and then.

List everything you currently remember about me, grouped into: work, home and life, preferences for how you answer, and anything else.

For each item, tell me whether it still seems accurate based on our recent chats. Mark anything that looks outdated or that I might not want stored, and tell me how to remove it.

Image creation and editing

Describe an image in the chat, or choose Images in the sidebar to start from a template or a sketch. To edit, select an image you made or uploaded, mark the area with the selection tool, and describe the change. The current model, ChatGPT Images 2.5, arrived in September 2026 with sharper detail and more precise editing. Free plans get a limited number. Everything you make is saved under Images.

Create an image for [purpose, e.g. a poster for a neighbourhood plant swap].

Format: [square / tall for a phone / wide for a slide].
Must include this exact text: "[text]".
Style: [e.g. flat illustration, warm colours, lots of white space].
Avoid: [e.g. stock-photo people, clutter, tiny text].

If any of this is unclear, ask me first. After you make it, suggest two changes that would make it more readable from across a room.
Tip

Edit in small steps. "Make the title bigger" then "change the background to light green" works better than five changes in one message.

Voice

Tap the Voice icon in the message bar to talk instead of type, on iPhone, Android or the web. You can share images during a voice chat. Free users get limited voice time. It's the best way to practise anything spoken: a presentation, a salary talk, a language. Audio clips are kept for 30 days with the transcript, and deleting the chat removes them.

Let's practise. You're [role, e.g. a hiring manager at a logistics company] and I'm [me, applying for a project coordinator role].

Ask one question at a time and wait for my answer. After each, give me one thing I did well and one specific improvement, briefly. After five questions, tell me my strongest and weakest answer.

For a full interview routine, see Practise a job interview with AI.

Study mode

Study mode turns ChatGPT into a tutor that asks questions, explains in steps and checks you've understood, instead of handing you the answer. On the web, type @study in the message box or search for "study" in the + menu. On mobile, open + and choose Study. It's on all plans. Delete the Study tag from the message box to switch it off.

I want to understand [topic, e.g. how compound interest works] well enough to [goal, e.g. explain it to my teenage son].

My current level: [what you already know]. Teach me step by step. Don't give me the full answer at once. Ask me a question after each step, and only move on when I've got it right. Finish with a 5-question quiz.

Shopping research

When you're comparing products, ChatGPT can build a personal buyer's guide. Select one or more products mentioned in a chat and choose Research. It asks about your needs and budget, then returns top picks, a comparison table and alternatives. OpenAI says these results are organic and separate from ads. Where available, clothing listings also have a Try on button.

Help me choose [product, e.g. a robot vacuum] for [situation, e.g. a 70 m² flat with wooden floors and a cat].

Budget: up to €[amount]. Must have: [list]. Don't care about: [list].

Ask me questions first if you need to. Then compare 3 options in a table (price, strengths, main weakness) and tell me which you'd pick and why.

Plugins and connected apps

Apps connect ChatGPT to services you use, such as Google Drive, Outlook, Microsoft Teams, Slack or Notion, so it can search and work with your information. Plugins bundle instructions and apps into reusable workflows. To set one up, open Plugins (some accounts still say Apps), pick one, select Install plugin or Connect, sign in and review the permissions. In a chat, type @ and the app's name, or choose it from the + menu. Availability depends on your plan and country.

@Google Drive Find the documents about [project or topic] from the last [two weeks].

Summarise what's been decided, what's still open and who is responsible for what. List the files you used with their names, so I can check them. If you're not sure a file is relevant, ask me rather than including it.
Heads-up

Connect only the apps you'll actually use, and read the permission screen. A connected app lets ChatGPT read what's in that account. You can disconnect any app later from the same place.

Scheduled tasks

Scheduled tasks let ChatGPT do something later or on repeat: a reminder, a daily briefing, a weekly price check. Open Scheduled and ask in plain words. Free and Go allow 3 active tasks (one-off or daily only), Plus 5, Business 10, Pro 15. Results arrive by push notification or email. Manage them under Settings, Notifications, Manage tasks.

Every Monday at 7:30, send me a short briefing:
- the weather for [city] this week, flagging rainy days,
- 3 news items about [your interest] from the past week, each with a one-line "why it matters" and the source,
- one reminder: [e.g. pay the cleaner on Friday].

Under 200 words, readable on my phone.

ChatGPT Work

Work is for jobs that take many steps and end in a deliverable. You describe the outcome, and it researches, analyses and builds documents, spreadsheets, presentations, reports or simple websites (OpenAI calls these Sites). It can use connected apps and the web, and in the desktop app your local files, with permission. It replaced the older "agent mode". Use the Chat and Work toggle in the desktop app, or the dropdown on web and mobile. It's on Plus and above, with limited access for Free and Go in the desktop app.

Create a [5-slide presentation / 2-page report / spreadsheet] on [topic] for [audience].

Purpose: [what the audience should decide afterwards].
Use: [the attached files / my notes below / public sources].
Must include: [e.g. a cost comparison and one recommendation].

Show me an outline and wait for my OK before you build it. Flag any number you couldn't verify.
Heads-up

Work can now act on websites you're signed in to. Give it a clear stopping point, such as "stop before anything is submitted or paid", and stay nearby for anything involving money or personal details.

Codex

Codex is OpenAI's agent for software. It's on every plan, Free included, and runs in the desktop app, at chatgpt.com/codex, in the VS Code editor and in a terminal. Running tasks on OpenAI's servers (Codex Cloud) needs Plus or above. Non-coders can still build small things with it.

I'm not a programmer. Build me [a single web page that shows my favourite recipes, with a search box / a script that renames the photos in a folder by date].

Keep it as simple as possible, in as few files as possible. Explain in plain English what each file does and exactly how I open or run it on [Mac / Windows]. Ask me questions before you start if anything is unclear.

Custom GPTs (on the way out)

Custom GPTs are mini versions of ChatGPT that someone set up for one job. OpenAI is retiring them. According to its retirement FAQ, creating new GPTs is planned to stop on 26 October 2026 and existing ones stop working on 11 December 2026, with plugins taking over. My advice: don't build a new GPT now. Put your instructions and files in a Project instead.

Extras on higher plans, and US-only features

  • Space and Pages (Pro, Business, Enterprise): documents you write and edit with ChatGPT and colleagues together.
  • Dots: always-on agents that keep working between your chats, for Pro users outside the EEA, Switzerland and the UK, and Business Premium seats.
  • Health and Finances: for connecting health records or bank accounts, US only so far. I'd be careful with both.

45 things to try, by life area

Short prompts to copy. Replace anything in [brackets], paste in what it needs, then follow up until it fits.

Work

  1. Prepare for a meeting. "I'm meeting [who] about [topic]. What should I aim to leave with, and how should I answer their likely objections?"
  2. Shrink a long thread. "Here's an email thread. Tell me in 5 lines what's agreed, what's open, and what I need to reply to." Paste the thread.
  3. Notes to actions. "Turn these meeting notes into a table: action, owner, deadline. Add a column for anything vague I should clarify."
  4. Decline politely. "Help me say no to this request. Keep the relationship warm, offer one small alternative, under 90 words." Paste the request.
  5. Write the update. "Turn these bullet points into a weekly update for my manager. Results first, then problems, then what I need from them."
  6. Document a process. "Turn my messy description of how I do [task] into numbered steps a new colleague could follow."
  7. Explain a spreadsheet. Upload it: "Give me the 3 most important patterns, one chart, and anything that looks like an error."

Writing

  1. Cut it down. "Make this 40% shorter without losing any facts. Then list what you removed."
  2. Match my voice. "Here are 3 things I wrote. Describe my style in 5 bullets, then rewrite the draft below in that style."
  3. Skim test. "Read this like someone who has 30 seconds. What do they take away, and what should I move to the top?"
  4. Clean up a voice note. "Turn this transcript of me talking into a clear email with a subject line. Keep my meaning, lose the rambling."
  5. Proofread in British English. "Check spelling, grammar and clarity. Show each change as before and after, and leave my tone alone."
  6. Tricky message. "I need to tell [person] that [hard news]. Give me a kind version and a direct version, each under 100 words."

Learning

  1. Explain like a good teacher. "Explain [topic] to a smart beginner with one everyday example. Then ask me 3 questions to check I got it."
  2. Language practice. "Let's chat in simple [German]. Correct my mistakes after each message, with a one-line explanation in English."
  3. A month of small steps. "Make a 4-week plan to learn the basics of [skill] in 20 minutes a day, with one task per day and how I'll know I did it."
  4. Read a chart. Upload a photo of a chart and ask: "What does this chart actually show, and what would be a misleading way to read it?"
  5. Book in 10 minutes. "Give me the 5 main ideas of [book], how they apply to [my situation], and the strongest criticism of it."
  6. Flashcards. "Make 15 flashcards from these notes. Question on the front, short answer on the back. Then quiz me on the hardest 5."

Money and admin

  1. Decode a letter. Photograph an official letter and ask: "What is this, what do I have to do, by when, and what happens if I don't?"
  2. Find the money leaks. "Here are my subscriptions with prices. Group them, total them per month and year, and suggest which to cancel or downgrade." See also the 20-minute money-leak audit.
  3. Compare two offers. "Compare these two [phone / energy] contracts in a table: total cost over 2 years, notice period and catches."
  4. Write a complaint. "Write a firm, polite complaint to [company] about [problem]. Include what I want as a fix and a reasonable deadline."
  5. Simple budget. "My take-home pay is €[amount] and these are my fixed costs. Build a monthly budget table with a savings line."
  6. Prepare questions. "I'm seeing a [tax adviser / lawyer / bank adviser] about [topic]. What should I bring, and what 8 questions should I ask?"
  7. Fill in a form. "Explain each field on this form in plain English and tell me which documents I need for each." Upload a photo, but cover any ID numbers first.

Home and life

  1. Fridge dinner. "Here's a photo of my fridge. Suggest 3 dinners I can make in 30 minutes, using what's there plus basic pantry items."
  2. Week of meals. "Plan 5 weeknight dinners for [number] people, [dietary needs], under €[amount] in total, with one shopping list grouped by aisle."
  3. Fix it. Take a photo and ask: "What is this part, what's probably wrong, and is this a DIY job or should I call someone?"
  4. Weekend away. "Plan 2 relaxed days in [city] for [who] who like [things]. One area per day, with a rain backup."
  5. Gift ideas. "Suggest 10 gift ideas for [person] who likes [interests], under €[amount]. Mix things to buy and things to do."
  6. Cleaning plan. "Make a weekly cleaning rota for a [size] flat shared by [number] people, about 20 minutes a day, fair to everyone."
  7. Hard conversation. "Help me plan a calm talk with [person] about [issue]: an opening line and how to respond if they get defensive."

Creating

  1. Ideas worth making. "Give me 15 post ideas about [topic] for [audience]. Mark the 3 most likely to start a conversation and say why."
  2. Hook rewrite. "Here's my opening line. Write 8 alternatives: some curious, some direct, some with a number. No clickbait."
  3. Carousel outline. "Turn this idea into a 7-slide carousel: one sentence per slide, a strong first slide and a clear last-slide action."
  4. Product photo. Upload a phone photo of your product and ask: "Put this on a clean light-grey background with soft shadow. Keep the product exactly as it is."
  5. Video script. "Write a 45-second video script about [topic]: a 3-second hook, 3 points, and an ending that asks a question."
  6. Personal card. "Write a birthday message for [person] who [two details about them]. Warm, a little funny, under 50 words."

Building

  1. Spreadsheet formula. "In Google Sheets, column A has dates and column B has amounts. Write a formula that totals each month, and explain it simply."
  2. Tracker template. "Design a simple job-application tracker: the columns, a status drop-down list, and 3 rules for keeping it up to date."
  3. Simple web page. "Write a one-page site for my [service]: headline, 3 benefits, prices, contact link. Explain how to put it online for free."
  4. Automation idea. "I do [repeated task] every week. Suggest 3 ways to automate it with no-code tools, from easiest to most powerful."
  5. Fix an error. "Here's the error message I get in [app or spreadsheet]. Explain what it means and give me the fix step by step."
  6. Plan a small app. "I want an app that [does X] for [who]. Ask me 5 questions, then write a one-page plan for the simplest first version."

Bigger project prompts

Longer prompts for jobs that take an evening, not a minute. Run each inside its own Project so files and decisions stay together, and keep going in the same chat.

The job-hunt command centre

I'm job hunting in Germany myself, and staying organised is the hard part. This sets up the base.

I'm looking for [role] jobs in [country or city], starting [when]. My CV is attached.

Step 1: Ask me up to 8 questions about my experience, what I want, what I don't want and my non-negotiables.
Step 2: Write a one-page "candidate brief": my strongest selling points, the 3 kinds of roles I fit best, and the gaps I should be ready to explain.
Step 3: Suggest 15 job titles and search terms I should use, including ones I might not have thought of.
Step 4: Suggest a weekly routine for applications and follow-ups of no more than 6 hours.

Use only facts from my CV and answers. Never invent experience.

For tailoring each application without sounding generic, see Tailor your CV with AI without sounding like AI.

The holiday planner

Plan a [number]-day trip to [place] for [who is going, ages if children], in [month].

Budget: about €[amount] in total, excluding flights. We like: [interests]. We don't like: [dislikes]. Pace: [relaxed / packed].

First ask me any questions you need. Then give me:
1. A day-by-day plan with one main area per day, so we're not criss-crossing the city.
2. A rough cost per day.
3. What to book in advance and how early.
4. A rainy-day plan.

Search the web for opening times and prices and tell me the date of each source.

The paperwork tamer

I have a pile of letters and bills I keep avoiding. I'll upload photos of them one at a time (I've covered account and ID numbers).

For each one:
- tell me what it is and who it's from, in one line,
- tell me what I need to do, by when, and roughly how long it'll take,
- mark it urgent, soon or file only.

When I say "done", give me a single checklist sorted by deadline, and a simple folder structure for keeping these organised from now on. This is general help, so tell me if any item needs a professional.

The four-week skill sprint

I want to get noticeably better at [skill, e.g. Excel / public speaking / basic German] in 4 weeks, with [minutes] a day.

My level now: [describe honestly]. My goal: [a concrete thing I want to be able to do at the end].

Build a 4-week plan. For each week: the focus, 5 daily tasks, one weekend challenge, and how I'll measure progress. Keep tasks small enough that I'll actually do them on a tired day.

Then, each day I come back to this chat and say "day [number]", teach and quiz me on that day's task in study-mode style.

The side-business test

I'm thinking about a side business: [idea]. I live in [country], can spend [hours] a week and €[amount] to test it.

Be honest, not encouraging. First ask me 5 questions. Then:
1. Describe the likely customer and the problem they'd pay to solve.
2. List the 3 biggest risks and a cheap 30-day test for each.
3. Suggest a first offer and a price to test.
4. Say what result after 30 days should make me stop.

This is general information, not legal or tax advice. Tell me what I'd need to check with a professional before I take money.
Tip

Pair a project prompt with a scheduled task. For the skill sprint, for example, ask for a daily reminder at the time you'll actually practise.

Common mistakes

  • One-line requests. "Write a cover letter" gets a generic one. Add the job ad, your CV and why you fit.
  • Taking the first answer. It's a draft. Two or three follow-ups usually improve it a lot.
  • Trusting numbers blindly. Ask for sources, open them, and redo important sums yourself.
  • One giant chat for everything. Very long chats get muddled. New topic, new chat. Ongoing topic, a Project.
  • Never checking memory. If it keeps assuming something old, like your previous job, look in your saved memories.
  • Paying too early. Upgrade for a specific limit you keep hitting, not a vague feeling.
  • Staying on the fast setting. For anything with numbers, logic or real consequences, pick a thinking level, or press Think on Free.
  • Following old tutorials. Canvas, agent mode, the Atlas browser and custom GPTs have been retired or are on their way out. If you can't find a button a guide mentions, that's probably why.

What ChatGPT can't do (or shouldn't)

The tool rarely tells you when it's out of its depth, so know the limits up front.

  • It doesn't know what it doesn't know. It fills gaps with plausible guesses. Asking it to say "I don't know" helps but doesn't fix this fully.
  • It's not a professional. On money, law, tax and health it gives general information. For decisions with real consequences, such as signing a contract, filing taxes or a symptom that worries you, see a qualified person and use ChatGPT to prepare your questions.
  • It can be too agreeable. Ask "what's wrong with this?" if you want real critique.
  • It can't check the real world. It can't confirm a landlord is legitimate or a shop is open today. Search helps, but sources go stale.
  • It shouldn't judge people. Don't ask it to rate someone's character or honesty from a photo or a few messages.

When it gets stuck

The answer is generic. Reply: "Ask me 5 questions that would make this specific to me", answer them, then ask again.

It keeps getting a fact wrong. Turn on search, paste the correct information, or upload the source and say "use only this document".

It forgets your instructions in a long chat. Ask for a summary of the key decisions so far, start a new chat and paste the summary in.

Too many bullet points, emojis or too much cheer. Turn down Headers & Lists, Emojis and Enthusiastic under Characteristics, and add a line to your custom instructions.

You hit a limit. ChatGPT tells you when it resets. Meanwhile, use the default model for simple questions, or finish in another assistant.

A feature from a tutorial isn't there. It may be retired, renamed, limited to some plans or not in your country yet. Search the OpenAI help centre or the release notes.

Questions people ask

Is the free version good enough?

For most everyday writing, explaining and planning, yes. Since August 2026 Free includes unlimited everyday text chats with GPT-5.6 Luna and a Think button for harder questions. You'll notice the limits on images, uploads, voice and deep research first. Upgrade when one of those gets in your way regularly.

Is ChatGPT better than Claude or Gemini?

They're close. ChatGPT is a strong all-rounder with good voice and image tools. I build with Claude a lot for long documents and small tools, and Gemini suits people who live in Google's apps. To try the alternative, start with Claude from zero.

Can I use it in German or another language?

Yes. Write in any major language and it replies in kind, or ask it to answer in a different language, which helps when learning.

Is it safe to paste work documents?

Check your employer's policy first. On personal plans, switch off "Improve the model for everyone" and remove names and confidential numbers. If your company provides ChatGPT Business or Enterprise, use that instead.

What happened to canvas, agent mode, Atlas and GPTs?

OpenAI retired several features in 2026. Canvas was removed from the newer chat models in May. Agent mode was replaced by ChatGPT Work. The Atlas browser stopped working on 9 August 2026, with its features moving into ChatGPT and Codex. Custom GPTs are being replaced by plugins and are scheduled to stop working on 11 December 2026.

Does ChatGPT remember everything I say?

Only if memory is on, and even then it stores selected details rather than everything. You can see and delete saved memories under Settings, Personalization, Memory, or use a temporary chat when you don't want anything remembered.

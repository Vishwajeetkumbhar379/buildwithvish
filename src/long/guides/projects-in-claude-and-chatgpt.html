If you keep pasting the same CV, brand notes or background into every new chat, this page is for you. A Project gives a topic its own home: standing instructions, the files that matter and every chat about it in one place. By the end you'll have one working Project in Claude or ChatGPT (or the Google equivalent), instructions you can copy for five common situations, and a clear idea of what Projects can't do. The first one takes about 30 minutes. Each one after that takes 10.

In short
  • A Project is a workspace with its own instructions, files and chats. Every new chat inside it starts with that context.
  • Claude and ChatGPT both offer Projects on their free plans, with limits. Google's closest matches are Gems and notebooks in Gemini.
  • The instructions matter more than the files. Say who you are, what the Project is for and how you want answers, in under 200 words.
  • Add only files you'd otherwise paste, with passwords, ID numbers and other people's details removed.

Before you start

You need a free account with at least one of the apps. Nothing here needs a paid plan, though paid plans give you more room. Have these ready:

  • A name for the Project and one sentence on what it's for.
  • Two to five files you keep pasting into chats: a CV, a plan, a syllabus, a set of notes.
  • Ten quiet minutes to write the instructions. That's the part that makes the difference.

If you're new to the apps themselves, start with Claude from zero or ChatGPT from zero and come back.

What a Project is (and isn't)

A normal chat starts from nothing, apart from any general memory or settings you've switched on. A Project is a folder with three things attached:

  • Instructions: standing notes the AI reads at the start of every chat in the Project. Think of a brief for an assistant who joins fresh each morning.
  • Files (also called knowledge or sources): documents the AI can refer to in any chat in the Project.
  • Chats: every conversation about the topic, kept together.

So a new chat inside a "Job search" Project already knows your CV, the roles you want and that you hate cover letters that open with "I am writing to apply". You just type the task.

A Project is not a custom-trained AI. The model doesn't change. It's the same assistant reading your notes and files before it answers, so it's only as good as what you put in. It also doesn't run on its own: it waits for you to open a chat.

Create a Project in Claude

This works on the web and in the desktop app.

  1. Click Projects in the left sidebar (or go to claude.ai/projects).
  2. Click + New Project.
  3. Give it a name and a short description. Claude can't see either, so they're labels for you. Anything the AI needs to know goes in the instructions.
  4. Click Set project instructions, paste your instructions (the next section helps you write them) and click Save instructions.
  5. In the knowledge area, click + to upload files or add text. If you've connected Google Drive, you can add Drive files to private Projects.
  6. Start a chat from inside the Project.

Details worth knowing, from Anthropic's page on managing Projects and its file upload page:

  • Free accounts can have up to five Projects. Archive or delete old ones from the ... menu in the top right of a Project.
  • Each file can be up to 30 MB. Project files are read as text (multimodal PDFs aside), so a photo of a letter may not come through well.
  • On paid plans, when you add more than Claude can hold at once, it switches to searching your files. Anthropic says this raises capacity by up to ten times.
  • Sharing a Project ("Can view" or "Can edit") is for Team and Enterprise plans.

Create a Project in ChatGPT

  1. In the sidebar, select New project, type a name and pick an icon if you like.
  2. Open the ... menu in the Project and choose Project settings. Paste your instructions there. They apply only inside this Project and override your general custom instructions.
  3. In the same settings, choose the memory option (see below) and save.
  4. Add files: upload documents, spreadsheets or images, or paste text. You can also paste links from supported apps such as Google Drive or Slack, but Drive files added this way don't stay in sync.
  5. Move existing chats in by dragging them onto the Project, or from a chat's menu choose Move to project.

OpenAI's Projects page lists the file limits: 5 per Project on Free, 25 on Go and Plus, 40 on Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu, with up to 10 uploaded at once. Projects can be shared on every plan, with collaborator limits that grow with the plan. Deleting a Project deletes its chats and instructions for good.

Tip

If you already have a long, useful conversation about the topic, move it into the Project rather than starting again. It picks up the Project's instructions and files from then on.

The Google versions: Gems and notebooks

Gems are reusable helpers. At gemini.google.com, open the sidebar, click Gems, then New Gem. Add a name and instructions, then under Knowledge click Add files to upload from your device or Drive. Click Save. According to Google's Gems page, Drive files stay up to date automatically, and you can share a Gem. Each chat with a Gem stands alone, though.

Notebooks in Gemini are the closest thing to a Project: chats, sources and instructions kept together. Open the sidebar, choose New notebook under "Notebooks", then Add sources. Instructions live under More, then Notebook settings. Notebooks sync with Gemini Notebook, the research app Google called NotebookLM until a rename in July 2026. Some features need Keep Activity switched on, and in the European Economic Area you may need to connect Gemini Notebook by hand (see Google's notebooks page). The free tier allowed 50 sources per notebook when I checked. More in Gemini for people who live in Google.

Write instructions that actually work

A Project with great files and no instructions behaves like a normal chat with attachments. Good instructions do four jobs:

  1. Context: who you are and the situation, in two or three sentences.
  2. Goal: what the Project is for and what "done" looks like.
  3. How to answer: length, format, tone, language, currency.
  4. Rules: what it keeps getting wrong, and what to do when it's unsure.

Keep them under about 200 words. Long instructions get followed less reliably, and you'll stop updating them.

Weak

You are a helpful career coach. Help me get a job. Be professional.

Better

I'm a marketing manager with 6 years' experience, applying for brand roles in Germany in English. My CV and two cover letters I like are in the files. For any job ad I paste: give a fit score out of 10 with three reasons, then the three CV changes that matter most. Plain British English. Never invent experience. If something's missing, ask me one question first.

The weak version gives the AI a costume. The better one gives it facts, a format and a rule that prevents the worst mistake. If writing instructions feels awkward, let the AI interview you:

I'm setting up a Project for [topic, e.g. my job search in Germany]. Interview me to write the Project instructions.

Ask one question at a time, no more than 8 in total. Cover: who I am, the goal, what a good answer looks like (length, format, tone), what I don't want, and what you should do when you're unsure.

Then write the instructions in under 180 words, addressed to you ("You are helping me..."), with a short list of rules at the end. Don't add anything I didn't tell you.
Tip

Add one rule every time the Project annoys you. If it writes essays, add "Answer in under 150 words unless I ask for more". After a month the instructions fit you properly.

Choose the files (and what to leave out)

The test: add a file if you'd otherwise paste it into chats more than once. Then:

  • Clean it first. Remove old versions and out-of-date parts. Two CVs in one Project means it may quote the wrong one.
  • Name it clearly. "CV_2026_English.pdf" beats "final_v3_new.pdf", and lets you say "use my CV file".
  • Prefer text to scans. Typed documents work better than photos.
Privacy

Don't put passwords, ID or passport numbers, bank details, tax numbers or other people's private information into a Project. Replace them with "[ID]" first. If you share a Project, everyone with access sees the files. For training and history settings, see Five privacy settings to check in your AI apps.

One file worth creating is a short summary of the Project itself. Run this after a few chats, check the result and add it to the files:

Based on everything in this Project so far, write a one-page summary I can save as a file called "Project summary".

Include: the goal, key facts about my situation, decisions we've made (with dates if you know them), what's still open, and the next three actions.

Use short headings and bullets. Mark anything you're unsure about with [check]. Don't invent anything that isn't in our chats or files.

Decide on memory and sharing

Memory is the app's habit of keeping notes about you across chats. Inside Projects:

  • Claude: with memory on, each Project keeps its own memory, separate from everything else (Anthropic's memory page). Switch it under Settings, then Memory.
  • ChatGPT: in Project settings choose Default memory or Project-only memory. Project-only keeps the Project to its own conversations. Changes can take a few hours, and shared Projects always use project-only memory.

I'd use project-only memory for anything sensitive, such as health admin or a job search. Before sharing any Project, reread its files for anything private.

Five Projects worth making

Each has instructions to paste. Fill in the brackets, cut what doesn't apply and add rules over time.

1. Job search

Files: CV, two cover letters you like, target roles and companies, a note on your situation (languages, notice period).

You're helping me with my job search. I'm a [job title] with [X] years in [field], looking for [type of role] in [country or city, remote or on-site]. I apply in [language]. My CV, example cover letters and target list are in the files.

When I paste a job ad:
1. Give a fit score out of 10 and the three biggest reasons.
2. List the three CV changes that would matter most, using only my real experience.
3. Ask before writing a cover letter.

Rules:
- Never invent skills, results or employers.
- Plain, specific language. No buzzwords.
- British spelling, amounts in €.
- If a requirement is a dealbreaker (language, visa, years), say so first.

For a full build with a tracker and interview prep, see Build a job-search assistant in a Claude Project.

2. Side business

Files: a one-page plan, prices, a customer description, three samples of your writing, a "decisions so far" note.

You're my assistant for [business name], a side business selling [product or service] to [customer] in [market]. I work on it about [hours] a week. The plan, prices, customer notes and voice samples are in the files.

How to help:
- Write in the voice shown in the samples: [e.g. friendly, direct, no exclamation marks].
- Keep ideas realistic for one person with [hours] a week and €[amount] a month.
- When I ask for ideas, give 3, ranked, each with the first step and rough cost.

Rules:
- Don't invent numbers about my sales or market. Ask, or tell me where to find them.
- Flag anything with a legal, tax or contract angle and suggest I check it with a professional.
- End longer answers with "Next step:" and one action.

3. Studying a course or skill

Files: syllabus, your notes, key readings, past assignment briefs.

You're my study partner for [course or skill], level [beginner, undergraduate, master's]. The syllabus and my notes are in the files. My exam or deadline is [date].

How to help:
- Explain using my course's terms and examples from the files first.
- When I ask to revise a topic, quiz me with 5 questions, one at a time, and wait for my answer before giving feedback.
- Keep explanations short and check I understood before moving on.

Rules:
- Don't write assignments for me. Help me plan, understand and improve my own drafts.
- If my notes and your knowledge disagree, say so and tell me to check the source.
- Say clearly when you're not sure.
Heads-up

Check your institution's rules on AI before using it for graded work. These instructions keep the AI in a tutor role, but the policy is what counts.

4. Health admin (the paperwork, not the medicine)

This one is for appointments, insurance letters, forms and questions to bring to a doctor. It is not for diagnosis or treatment, and the instructions say so. Files: keep them minimal: an appointments list and any letter you need explained, with insurance and ID numbers removed.

You help me organise health admin for [me, or my household]: tracking appointments, explaining letters and forms in plain language, drafting emails to clinics or my insurer, and preparing questions for my doctor.

Rules:
- Never diagnose, suggest treatments or comment on medication. If I ask a medical question, help me turn it into a clear question for my doctor or pharmacist.
- If something sounds urgent, tell me to contact a doctor or emergency services straight away.
- When explaining a letter or bill, put deadlines and what I need to do first.
- Write emails in [language], polite and short.
- Remind me to remove ID or insurance numbers if I paste them.

This is general information, not medical advice: for symptoms, results or medication, speak to a doctor or pharmacist. Health details are very sensitive, so I'd use project-only memory, skip uploading medical records and not share this Project.

5. Home and life admin

Files: rental and utility contracts (account numbers removed), a household task list, a budget sheet, notes on any move or renovation.

You help me run home admin for [household, e.g. two adults in a rented flat in (city)]. Contracts, the budget and our task list are in the files.

How to help:
- For any contract or offer, pull out: monthly cost in €, notice period, renewal date and anything unusual.
- For letters, write in [language], firm and polite, with the reference numbers I give you.
- For planning, give a short list ordered by deadline.

Rules:
- Don't guess at legal rights. If something depends on tenancy or consumer law, say so and suggest who to ask.
- Use only figures from the files or from me. Mark estimates clearly.

Contract points are general information, not legal advice. For a dispute or real money at stake, ask a professional or your local consumer advice service.

The first chat in any Project

Before we start: tell me in 5 bullet points what you understand about me and this Project from the instructions and files. Then list anything that seems missing, out of date or contradictory, and ask me up to 3 questions that would help you do better work here.

If the summary is wrong, fix the instructions or files, not the chat. Every future chat inherits those.

What Projects can't do

  • They don't learn on their own. If an answer is wrong, the fix is a better instruction or file.
  • Big Projects get searched, not read in full. The app can miss a detail buried in file 34. Point it at the right file by name.
  • They can misquote your files. For a deadline, figure or contract term, ask for the exact quote and check it yourself.
  • Files may not update. ChatGPT says Drive links added to a Project don't sync. Gems do update from Drive. In Claude, the safe habit is to re-upload a file when it changes.
  • Scheduled tasks don't always see them. OpenAI notes that a scheduled task created in a Project can't read the Project's files.
  • They aren't a filing system. Keep originals in your own folders. A Project is a working copy.

When it gets stuck

  • It ignores your instructions. Shorten them, put the key rules first, phrase them as commands, then start a new chat.
  • It quotes an old file. Delete the old version. Duplicates are the most common cause of wrong answers.
  • It can't find something you uploaded. Ask "Which files can you see in this Project? List their names." Re-upload anything missing, as text if it was a scan.
  • Other topics bleed in. In ChatGPT, switch to project-only memory. In Claude, review what's stored under Settings, then Memory.
  • You hit a limit. Archive finished Projects, merge small files into one, or split a sprawling Project in two.

Questions people ask

Do I need a paid plan?

No. Claude's free plan allows up to five Projects and ChatGPT's free plan allows unlimited Projects with five files each. Paid plans add more files and, in Claude, a search mode for very large file sets.

How are Project instructions different from my general settings?

General settings apply to every chat. Project instructions apply only inside that Project, and in ChatGPT they override your general ones. Keep always-true facts about you in general settings and topic rules in the Project.

Is a Gem the same as a Project?

Not quite. A Gem has instructions and files, but each chat with it stands alone. Notebooks in Gemini are closer to Projects because they keep chats together.

Should I put everything into one big Project?

No. One Project per goal works better. A mixed Project gives the AI conflicting context and rules that don't fit. Three to five active Projects is plenty for most people.

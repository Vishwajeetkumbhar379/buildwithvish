Who I am

I spent more than four years on the brand side of influencer marketing and brand management. I worked on 850+ creator deals and led teams. A lot of that job was briefs, shortlists, reports and the same careful work done by hand, week after week.

Then I moved to Germany to do an MBA and started building practical AI tools and workflows. I'm not an engineer, and I don't write code by hand. I describe what I need, AI drafts it, and I make the decisions. I'm also job hunting in Germany, so one of the first things I built was a job-search agent for my own search.

Most of my building happens in Claude, using Projects, Skills and connectors. When something turns out useful, I publish it as a small open-source tool on GitHub so anyone can use it.

Why this newsletter exists

Most AI advice is written for people who already like tech. I'm writing for the person who tried an AI chat once, got a bland answer and closed the tab.

That bland answer is rarely the tool's fault alone. It usually comes from two habits: a vague request, and stopping at the first reply. Both are fixable in minutes, and neither needs code. That's what Build Notes is about: small, practical habits that turn AI into something you use every week for real work.

Prompt of the week

Start with your to-do list. This prompt sorts it into what AI can draft for you, what you should do yourself, and what can go. Paste your real list. Messy is fine.

Here's everything on my list this week: [paste your list, messy is fine].

Sort it into a table with three columns:
1. AI can draft this: for each item, write the exact prompt I should use.
2. I do this myself: add one line on why AI shouldn't do it.
3. Drop, delay or hand off: say to whom or until when.

If an item is unclear, ask me about it before sorting. Don't guess.

Finish with the one task that matters most by Friday, and why.
Privacy

Before you paste, take out passwords, bank details, ID numbers and other people's private information. "Call A. about the invoice" gives the AI all it needs.

Try this today

Run the prompt with this week's real list. Then pick one item from the "AI can draft this" column and run the prompt it wrote for you. Give the whole thing about 15 minutes.

When the draft comes back, read it with one question in mind: what would I need to change before I'd send or use this? Write the answer down in a line. That gap between "almost" and "usable" is where most of the useful AI skills live, and it's exactly what the next two issues are about.

My take

The "I do this myself" column matters as much as the first one. AI is good at drafts and first passes. Decisions, relationships and anything you'll be judged on still need you.

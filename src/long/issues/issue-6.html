A common complaint about AI: "It doesn't get me." Fair enough. In a fresh chat it knows nothing about you, so you either explain yourself again every time or get answers written for a stranger. This week's fix takes about 30 minutes, once: a small folder of four files that tells AI who you are.

The four files

FileWhat goes in itUpdate
about-meWho you are, what you do, your goals and your limits (time, budget, the country you live in)Rarely
voiceHow you write: sentence length, words you use and avoid, a couple of short samplesRarely
current3 to 5 things you're working on, each with a goal, a deadline and what's blocking itMonthly
preferencesHow you like answers: lead with the answer, keep it short, tables for comparisons, say when unsureWhen something annoys you

Plain documents are fine. Keep each one to about a page. Short and specific works better than long and complete, because the AI has to apply all of it to every answer.

How to build it

  1. Let AI interview you for about-me. Ask it to interview you one question at a time about your work, goals and constraints, then write the file in short bullet points. Answering questions is quicker than writing about yourself.
  2. Build voice from your own writing. Paste three things you wrote and were happy with, and ask for a voice guide: traits, words you use, words you avoid. Make AI write in your voice goes much deeper.
  3. Write current by hand. It's the shortest file, and only you know what's really on your plate.
  4. Write preferences from your pet peeves. Every answer that annoys you is a line for this file. "Stop opening with a compliment" counts.
  5. Put the files where the AI can read them. Claude and ChatGPT both have Projects: a space with its own files and instructions. Add the four files there and start your chats inside it. In other apps, attach the files at the start of a chat.
  6. Test it. Ask: "What do you know about me, and what should I focus on this week?" If something's wrong, fix the file, not the chat.

Why "current" is the file that matters

About-me and voice barely change. Current goes stale fast, and a stale current file can be worse than none: the AI keeps steering you towards a project you finished or dropped weeks ago, and you won't always notice.

It's also the file that makes answers feel personal. "Help me plan my week" means something very different when the AI knows you have an exam on the 20th and a half-finished website. So the habit that keeps the whole folder useful is small: once a month, refresh current. Five minutes.

Privacy

Keep these files boring on purpose. No passwords, ID or passport numbers, bank details or health information, and nothing private about other people. "Partner, two kids, free evenings are rare" is all the context the AI needs. It's also worth checking your app's data settings: Five privacy settings to check.

Prompt of the week

Set a monthly reminder, then paste this with last month's current file. The AI asks the questions, so you don't have to remember what changed.

Here's my "current" file from last month: [paste it].

Help me update it. Ask me 5 questions, one at a time, about what I finished, what I dropped, what's new and what's blocking me now. Wait for each answer before asking the next question.

Then rewrite the file:
- 3 to 5 current projects, each with one line for the goal, the deadline and what's blocking it
- remove anything finished or dropped
- under one page, in short bullet points

Finally, list what you removed so I can check nothing important went missing.

Try this today

Write just the current file. Open a blank note and list three to five things on your plate, one line each: goal, deadline, blocker. That's about 10 minutes.

Paste it at the top of your next AI chat, before your actual question, and compare the answer with what you usually get. Then put a monthly reminder in your calendar called "refresh current", with the prompt above saved in the reminder's notes.

This week's idea: an AI fills every gap in your request with the most average guess. Leave out who it's for, and it writes for everyone. Leave out the length, and it picks a middle one. That's why so many answers feel fine and useless at the same time.

I saw this on the creator side long before AI. In creator campaigns, most problems start with a vague brief. Send "make something fun about our new product" and you get something fun and wrong. The creator did nothing strange: they filled the gaps with their best guess. AI does the same, only faster and with more confidence.

The six boxes

The fix is the brief I used for creators, rewritten for AI. Six boxes, a line or two in each:

BoxWhat goes in it
GoalOne outcome you could check afterwards: "After reading this, [who] will [do what]."
AudienceOne typical reader: what they already know and what they care about right now.
ContextWhat you know that the AI doesn't: background, what you tried, what flopped.
ConstraintsLimits as numbers and named words: "under 120 words", "no exclamation marks".
FormatThe exact shape: a table with named columns, three options, an email with a subject line.
ExampleSomething close to what you want, plus what to copy and what to leave alone.

If you only have time for two, fill Context and Example. Context is the detail only you know. Example shows tone, length and structure in one go, which no list of adjectives can do.

The line that makes AI ask first

End every brief with: "Ask me up to 3 questions before you start." AI chat tools are built to answer straight away, even when your request has holes. This line gives permission to do what a good professional does with a fuzzy brief: ask. Three is enough to close the big gaps and few enough that you'll actually answer them.

Reply in one message, numbered to match. Short answers are fine, and "your call" is a fine answer. The questions are also a free check on you. If the AI asks something you thought was obvious, that box was emptier than you realised.

What the difference looks like

Say you run a small pottery studio and you're launching your first six-week evening course.

Weak

Write an email about my new pottery course.

Better

Goal: fill 8 places on a 6-week Tuesday course. Audience: people who did a 2-hour taster and found the wheel hard. Context: afterwards they kept asking how to actually get good. Constraints: under 150 words, price stated clearly, one link.

The weak version gets a cheerful email any studio could send, with "Exciting news!" in the subject line and no price. The better one can open with the exact question people asked after tasters. That opening only exists because you put it in the Context box.

Prompt of the week

Here's the whole brief as a template. Save it somewhere you can reach in two taps. Delete a box that truly doesn't apply, but think twice before deleting Context or Example.

GOAL: [one clear outcome: "after reading this, [who] will [do what]"]

AUDIENCE: [who it's for, what they already know, what they care about right now]

CONTEXT: [background, what you've tried, what didn't work, why now; paste real material where you can]

CONSTRAINTS: [length, tone, language, words to avoid, must-include points, deadline]

FORMAT: [exactly how the answer should be laid out: table and columns, number of options, bullets, email with subject line]

EXAMPLE: [paste something close to what you want; say what to copy (tone, length, structure) and what NOT to copy]

Ask me up to 3 questions before you start. If nothing is unclear, say so and go ahead.
Heads-up

A good brief shapes the answer. It doesn't make the facts true, so still check names, numbers, prices and anything legal. And keep passwords, ID numbers, bank details and other people's private information out of every box.

Try this today

Pick one real task you'd normally fire off as a one-liner: an email, a post, a short plan. Run the one-liner first and keep the answer. Then open a new chat, fill the six boxes (about 10 minutes) and send it with the magic line at the end. Answer its questions, then put the two results side by side.

Last step: whatever the AI asked you, add the answer to your saved template. Next time you'll give it up front, and the brief gets faster every week.

One honest limit: small things don't need six boxes. Converting €80 into dollars is a one-liner. Save the full brief for anything you'll send, publish or act on.
